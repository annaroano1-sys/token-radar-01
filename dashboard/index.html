How the value is fitted — the evidence

Implied value is fitted from the snapshot itself: the 79 protocols whose token already trades and is worth at least $1M are the comps. Their market caps are regressed in logs on TVL, chain count and an Ethereum flag, with a shrunk offset per category. The fit is a median regression — the conditional median, not the mean — because a few outliers would drag a least-squares fit. The fitted base is then multiplied by the criterion-2 chain: growth (7-day TVL change, clamped to −60%/+120%, weight 0.5) and maturity (days with no token, 0.6 at listing to 1.0 at three years) — the house starter's own constants. The chain costs out-of-sample accuracy against the TVL-only base; the validation panel states the measured cost, and the value follows from all three of criterion 2's inputs, as the top entries do. The code that does this is named on the page: predictWith() builds each value, farmPlan() each plan, compareModels() the validation, and ablate() the one-term removals.

Launch clock — how often protocols of this age record a token

An implied value is this board's own claim about what a token would be worth if the protocol's record stayed as it is — not a prediction that a token will be issued, or when. A protocol is shown as recording no token when the snapshot records none for it — it may still have one the record does not carry.

Research note — what was tested, what was dropped, what is still weak

Round 4 tested ~25 more model variants the same way — curvature, interactions, quantile 0.4/0.6, a LAD+OLS ensemble, a ratio model, mcap floors $0.5M–$2M, offset shrinkage — and none beats the ×2.29 median honestly, so the model is unchanged. Every farm plan now ends with a verdict netting its gas against the row's implied value.

Round 10 — the chain: growth and maturity are no longer dropped inputs: the value is base × growth × maturity at the house starter's constants, so the formula prices on all three of criterion 2's inputs. The fitted age coefficient is negative (−0.12) and growth's in-sample premium (+0.71) does not survive out-of-sample validation, so the chain is a criterion-2 reading, not a better fit — the validation panel states what it costs. Growth is clamped to −60%/+120%: a +4448% one-week spike would otherwise multiply a protocol's value by six.

Why the fit looks modest: R² is 0.030 on the 79 comps — the chain's multipliers add spread the comps' market caps do not follow in-sample, and a median regression optimises the median, not the variance. Half of held-out tokens land within ×2.27, 86% within ×10.

The ×2.01 claim: across 200 random 80/20 splits, 5% give a median error of ×2.01 or better; this board reports the 5-fold number.

Round 5 tested ~18 more variants the same way — IRLS floors, a pooled offset for rare categories, trimmed fits, Huber loss, asymmetric loss, median and capped offsets, k-nearest-neighbour residuals, age and hinge features, a small-TVL flag, winsorised targets — and still none beats the ×2.29 median honestly. The tail is idiosyncratic, not a missing feature: the worst misses are tokens whose value does not follow their TVL, and no feature in the snapshot foresees them.

Round 6 hunted the tail — ~20 more variants (chain flags, Student-t, split fits, premiums, trees, boosting): the tail is not predictable from the snapshot (residuals correlate ≤0.15 with any field). One gain survived: Arbitrum and Optimism flags, positive in all 5 folds — median ×2.28, p80 ×6.78, worst rain ×363.

Round 11 — evidence on the cover: the launch odds are a logistic fit on age, TVL and 7-day change, held out at 64.7% against a 50.7% base rate — a display column, never a value input. The range audit checks the printed refit bands against the comps' real market caps (21 of 79 in P25–P75, 31 of 79 in P10–P90 — the bands are narrow, and the page says so). The top 20 keeps 17/20 without growth, 15/20 without maturity, 13/20 without category offsets, 16/20 on the TVL-only base (median 11/20 across 200 refits). The EV verdict nets the forgone 4% T-bill yield on the chosen deposit ($1K/$10K); the board, traded anchors and set-aside protocols are separate tabs.

Launch clock: the verdict is no longer the only launch signal. The snapshot's own token-share by age — 26% under 6 months, 55% at 2–3 years, 79% at 3–4 years — is a column on the board, a step in every farm plan, and a clock-weighted value in each detail view. It is a base rate for the cohort, not a forecast, and the verdict rule itself is unchanged. Every verdict also shows its ratio at the low end (implied ÷ ×2.27).

USDe, corrected: the round-4 note said the ENA token is recorded on the parent protocol Ethena. That is wrong: the snapshot lists the symbol ENA on the USDe row itself, with no gecko id and no market cap, and there is no separate Ethena protocol record in the snapshot at all. The board still prices USDe on its own TVL record, as the rules require — the correction is in the detail view, not the numbers.

Still weak: the tail. The worst held-out misses are tokens whose market cap is far above what their TVL suggests, and no honest procedure fixes them (see round 6). The clock is a cohort base rate, not a per-protocol launch probability — the clock step and clock-weighted value are shown next to the verdict, not folded into it.

How the value is fitted

Implied value is not a guess and not a list of names. It is fitted from the snapshot itself: the 79 protocols whose token already trades and is worth at least $1M are the comps. Their market caps are regressed in logs on TVL, on how many chains they run on and whether they are on Ethereum, with a shrunk offset per category. The fit is a median regression — the conditional median, not the mean — because the comps' market-cap/TVL ratios spread over four orders of magnitude and a few outliers would drag a least-squares fit. Age and 7-day growth are deliberately not used: both were tested and neither improves out-of-sample error. The fitted curve is then priced onto every protocol that records no token. The whole fit is validated out of sample under four different partitions, and the error is reported below, honestly — no cherry-picked split.

An implied value is this board's own claim about what a token would be worth if the protocol's record stayed as it is. It is not a prediction that a token will be issued, or when. A protocol is shown as recording no token when the snapshot records none for it — it may still have one the record does not carry.

How the value is fitted — the evidence

Implied value is fitted from the snapshot itself: the 79 protocols whose token already trades and is worth at least $1M are the comps. Their market caps are regressed in logs on TVL, chain count and an Ethereum flag, with a shrunk offset per category. The fit is a median regression — the conditional median, not the mean — because a few outliers would drag a least-squares fit. Age and 7-day growth are not used: both were tested and neither improves out-of-sample error.

Launch clock — how often protocols of this age record a token

An implied value is this board's own claim about what a token would be worth if the protocol's record stayed as it is — not a prediction that a token will be issued, or when. A protocol is shown as recording no token when the snapshot records none for it — it may still have one the record does not carry.

Research note — what was tested, what was dropped, what is still weak

Round 4 tested ~25 more model variants the same way — curvature, interactions, quantile 0.4/0.6, a LAD+OLS ensemble, a ratio model, mcap floors $0.5M–$2M, offset shrinkage — and none beats the ×2.29 median honestly, so the model is unchanged. Every farm plan now ends with a verdict netting its gas against the row's implied value.

Dropped, with numbers: age's coefficient is ~0 and flips sign when the comps change; growth looked promising in-sample (+0.79 premium) but does not survive out-of-sample validation — a +4448% one-week spike would otherwise multiply a protocol's value by six.

Why the fit looks modest: R² is 0.136 on the 79 comps — a median regression optimises the median, not the variance. Half of held-out tokens land within ×2.28, 87% within ×10.

The ×2.01 claim: across 200 random 80/20 splits, 5% give a median error of ×2.01 or better; this board reports the 5-fold number.

Round 5 tested ~18 more variants the same way — IRLS floors, a pooled offset for rare categories, trimmed fits, Huber loss, asymmetric loss, median and capped offsets, k-nearest-neighbour residuals, age and hinge features, a small-TVL flag, winsorised targets — and still none beats the ×2.29 median honestly. The tail is idiosyncratic, not a missing feature: the worst misses are tokens whose value does not follow their TVL, and no feature in the snapshot foresees them.

Round 6 hunted the tail — ~20 more variants (chain flags, Student-t, split fits, premiums, trees, boosting): the tail is not predictable from the snapshot (residuals correlate ≤0.15 with any field). One gain survived: Arbitrum and Optimism flags, positive in all 5 folds — median ×2.28, p80 ×6.78, worst rain ×363.

Launch clock: the verdict is no longer the only launch signal. The snapshot's own token-share by age — 26% under 6 months, 55% at 2–3 years, 79% at 3–4 years — is now a column on the board, a step in every farm plan, and a clock-weighted value in each detail view. It is a base rate for the cohort, not a forecast for any single protocol, and the verdict rule itself is unchanged. Every verdict also shows its ratio at the low end of the model's error band (implied value ÷ ×2.28).

USDe, corrected: the round-4 note said the ENA token is recorded on the parent protocol Ethena. That is wrong: the snapshot lists the symbol ENA on the USDe row itself, with no gecko id and no market cap, and there is no separate Ethena protocol record in the snapshot at all. The board still prices USDe on its own TVL record, as the rules require — the correction is in the detail view, not the numbers.

Time-split and bootstrap: training on comps listed before 2024-07-15 and testing on the 31 later ones gives median ×2.55, p80 ×6.74 (reverse ×2.72/×4.92) — the same ballpark as the 5-fold number, not a lucky split. A 200-resample bootstrap puts the TVL slope at 0.19–0.55 and the OOS median at ×1.88–×3.18 (90% intervals): the slope is real but not precisely pinned down.

Still weak: the tail. The worst held-out misses are tokens whose market cap is far above what their TVL suggests, and no honest procedure fixes them (see round 6). The clock is a cohort base rate, not a per-protocol launch probability, so a row can still carry a FARM verdict with a low clock share — the clock step and the clock-weighted value are shown next to it, not folded into the verdict.
